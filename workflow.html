3. Case Study : Fair Scholarship allowances

3.1 Description

The Government of a country want to implement an algorithm to decide automatically who will benefit from a 5 year scholarship allowance. It has 500000 allowances and its duty is to assign them in the most possible fair way. Also taking into account that money must not be wasted and candidates should finish their career. For this task they come to me and my playbook.

3.2 Applying historical module

The first task to assess fairness in such an algorithm is to feed this problem as input of the Historical Context Module and answer questions :

3.2.1. Domain and Application Context

  • q1 : Application Domain Identification. What specific domain will this system operate in (e.g., lending, hiring, healthcare, criminal justice)? What function will it serve within this domain?
  • a1 : The application domain is scholarship money assignment. It will serve the function of assign scholarship ressources in the most fair possible way.
  • q2: What documented patterns of discrimination exist in this domain? Consider both explicit policies (e.g., redlining in housing) and implicit practices.
  • a2 : It has been shown by statistics than scholarships are assigned to people living in the cities, because universities are also in the cities. It has been also shown that people between 20-35 got the scholarhips, and people above 35 tend to be excluded.

3.2.2 Data And Representation Analysis :

3.2.2.1. Historical Data Sources.

  • Q) What historical data sources will inform this system? How were these datasets collected, and what historical contexts shaped their collection?
  • A) Data Sources feeding the system will be people that have been choosen for scholaship, with their attributes and a boolean if he could finish or not.

3.2.2.2 Categorical Formation.

  • How have relevant categories (e.g., race, gender, creditworthiness) been defined historically in this domain? How have these definitions changed over time?
  • During history genders have been only Male and Female, however, some years ago government create a figure "mother head of family" in order to highlight the vulnerability of this population.

3.2.2.3 Measurement Approaches.

  • How have key variables historically been measured in this domain? Might these measurements encode historical biases?
  • Measures have been focused in obtained grades, keeping aside other important meassures like quality of the job obtaining after and income. These could evidence a bias because students with better ressources could get better grades .

3.2.3. Previous Technological Systems.

  • q1. What previous technological systems have served similar functions in this domain? How did these systems perpetuate or challenge existing inequities?
  • a1. Before, scholarship assignation was done filling a in paper application, followed by a comitte review, decisions were done by voting.These system privilege people in the cities, because was easy for them go physically to fill in papers.
  • q2. Technological Amplification. How might an automated system potentially amplify existing historical biases compared to previous systems? What new capabilities introduce additional risks?
  • a2. Because of the nature of data, we will count with data of people between 20-35, the other age categories will be excluded.

    4. Validation framework

    4.1 Metrics follow up

    Before and After each algorithm deployment, team must check the set of metrics defined for fairness, it must be clear that choosen metrics are improving over time.

    4.2 Intersectional surveillance

    Team must check algorithm outputs for people in intersections of two or more protected attributes and should observe and report if discrimination patterns arises
